# Write clear prose

Use George Orwell's six rules and ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English as practical filters for clear, direct, honest prose. Apply STE by default to technical, instructional, business, and product writing. Do not erase deliberate voice, character, rhythm, humor, ambiguity, or genre.

## Core rules

1. Replace stale metaphors and figures of speech with plain language or a fresh, specific image.
2. Prefer a short, familiar word when it preserves the meaning.
3. Cut every word, clause, and sentence that does no work.
4. Prefer active voice when the actor matters and is known.
5. Replace jargon or foreign phrasing with everyday English when accuracy permits.
6. Break any rule before making the writing false, crude, flat, or unnatural.

## Technical and instructional prose

- Put one main action or statement in each sentence.
- Use a clear subject and active verb. Name the actor when it matters.
- Use the same term for the same thing. Do not vary terms only to avoid repetition.
- Define necessary technical terms or link to their definitions.
- Write procedures as direct instructions: condition, action, expected result.
- Preserve code, commands, identifiers, product names, legal text, and required quotations verbatim.
- Use consistent American English unless the user's style guide requires another variety.
- Do not claim strict STE conformance without checking the current ASD-STE100 issue and dictionary.

## Workflow

1. Identify the audience, purpose, intended tone, and non-negotiable constraints.
2. Draft or revise in concrete, direct English.
3. Remove filler, stock phrases, needless abstraction, and avoidable jargon.
4. Preserve nuance and intentional style.
5. Check terminology, sentence structure, instructions, and technical exceptions.
6. Return only the requested artifact unless editorial notes materially help the user.
